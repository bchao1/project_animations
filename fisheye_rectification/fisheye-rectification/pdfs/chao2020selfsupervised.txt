ABSTRACT
To rectify ﬁsheye distortion from a single image, we advance
self-supervised learning strategies and propose a unique
deep learning model of Fisheye GAN (FE-GAN). Our FE-
GAN learns pixel-level distortion ﬂow from sets of ﬁsheye
distorted images and distortion-free ones (but not requir-
ing such correspondences), with unique cross-rotation and
intra-warping consistency introduced. With such novel self-
supervised learning techniques, our FEGAN is able to recover
the distortion-free image directly from the single ﬁsheye im-
age input.
Our experiments quantitatively and qualitative
conﬁrm the effectiveness and robustness of our proposed
model, which performs favorably against recent GAN-based
image translation models.
Index Terms— ﬁsheye camera, image rectiﬁcation, gen-
erative adversarial network, deep learning, self-supervised
learning
1. INTRODUCTION
Fisheye cameras are widely used in a variety of computer
vision tasks. With a large ﬁeld of view (FOV), the images
captured by ﬁsheye cameras contain more content informa-
tion, favorable for users with particular viewing preferences.
Unfortunately, images taken by ﬁsheye cameras often suffer
from large geometric distortions, especially around the outer
regions of the image. Such undesirable properties might de-
grade the performance in recognition and segmentation, since
the pin-hole camera model assumption cannot be held. There-
fore, it is desirable to be able to rectify such artifacts, so that
highly structured physical properties of the image content can
be properly observed and processed.
Early works on ﬁsheye image rectiﬁcation often require
intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters for calibration,
while such parameters can be estimated from multi-view
images [1, 2]. However, it might not be always applicable
to collect multi-view images for a scene of interest. Thus,
how to address such single-image rectiﬁcation tasks becomes
a more challenging task.
Fitzgibbon [3] proposed a divi-
sion model to approximate ﬁsheye distortion in images with
fewer parameters.
To estimate the distortion parameters,
Bukhari et al. [4] detected curved-arcs, while Aleman-Flores
et al. [5] utilized an improved Hough Transform to achieve
so. Many works have followed this “plumb-line” assump-
tion to estimate distortion parameters from distorted straight
lines [4, 6, 7]. Nevertheless, the above methods are based on
the pre-determined hand-crafted low-level features (e.g., edge
detection, Hough Transform, etc.), which might have limited
abilities in describing image content.
With the recent advances of deep learning, alternative ap-
proaches to image rectiﬁcation based on deep convolutional
neural networks (CNNs) have shown promising results. Yin
et al. [8] predicted distortion parameters, guided by seman-
tic segmentation maps to rectify the ﬁsheye images. Xue et
al. [9] leveraged CNNs for line segment annotations, and fo-
cused on transforming curved arcs into straight lines. Li et
al. [10] regressed the pixel-wise ﬂow between ﬁsheye and
distortion-free images to re-sample the distorted pixels. How-
ever, most existing learning-based methods for image rectiﬁ-
cation require supervision (i.e., ground truth distortion-free
images, distortion parameters, line segments, segmentation
maps, ground truth ﬂow, or paired image data) [8, 9, 10].
The above assumptions and requirements might not be prac-
tical when training deep learning models for real-world ap-
plications. Furthermore, all of these works assume that there
is only one ﬁsheye distortion model, and thus choose to re-
cover its parameters. In real-life scenarios, however, we may
have no prior knowledge about the distorted ﬁsheye images.
Assuming the use of a single camera model for image rectiﬁ-
cation might not be sufﬁcient.
To overcome the above problems, we propose an end-
to-end deep learning framework based on Generative Adver-
sarial Networks (GANs) [11], Fisheye GAN (FE-GAN). Our
FE-GAN does not require ground truth camera parameters or
corresponding distortion-free image during training. By ad-
vancing self-supervised learning (SSL) strategy and ideas of
optical ﬂow [12], our model implicitly learns intrinsic camera
parameters for single-image ﬁsheye rectiﬁcation.
The contributions of our work are highlighted as follows:
• We are among the ﬁrst to propose self-supervised deep
learning models for ﬁsheye image rectiﬁcation.
• By utilizing GAN-based models, our proposed FE-
GAN advances self-supervised learning (SSL) strate-

2.1. Problem Deﬁnition and Notations

we aim at learning a mapping function between the dis-

image domain Y . During training, unpaired training images

Our proposed deep self-supervised learning (SSL) frame-

2.2. Fisheye GAN (FE-GAN)
Overall Architecture
As shown in Figure 1, our proposed deep learning framework
for ﬁsheye image rectiﬁcation is based on Generative Adver-
sarial Networks (GANs). Our generator G : X→Y aims at
recovering the distortion-free images Y from images X cap-
tured by ﬁsheye cameras. To be more speciﬁc, the genera-
tor part of our network consists of a ﬂow prediction module
U : X→F and a warping module W : (X, F)→Y . We note
that, the ﬂow prediction module U ﬁrst takes an input dis-
torted image xi∈MH∗W ∗3 and outputs a ﬂow map U(xi) =
fi∈MH∗W ∗2. As for the warping module W, it is applied to
recover the rectiﬁed image using the predicted ﬂow, and thus
the distortion-free predicted output is exi = W(xi, fi).
On the other hand, the discriminator D in our pro-
posed framework consists of two unique modules, i.e., D =
(Dadv, Dcls). Dadv aims to distinguish between {G(x) = exi}
and {y}, while Dcls identiﬁes the output images as distorted
ones or those with distortion free. Note that Dcls is trained
in an adversarial fashion, which views distortion-free images
as real and the generated outputs as distorted ones. The ob-
jectives of the above adversarial and classiﬁcation-oriented
discriminators are described below:

The above cross-rotation consistency utilizes data-augmentation
like techniques for recovering image or distortion ﬂow out-
puts of interest, without observing the resulting ground truth
information. We now advance the intrinsic properties of ﬁsh-

consistency for the purpose of self-supervised learning.

through the image center, the generated distortion ﬂow fi of

• Radial-symmetric: The distortion ﬂow for each pixel

image center, while the ﬂow magnitudes for equidistant
pixels across the image center remain the same.

izontal and vertical directions for adjacent pixels would
be small.

3. EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS
3.1. Datasets
We evaluate our method on three image datasets constructed
from MCindoor20000 [14], Wireframe [15], and LSUN
Churches [16]. Following [8, 9, 10], we apply the ﬁsheye
transform deﬁned in [17] with randomly determined param-
eters to synthesize 1000 ﬁsh-eye images for each dataset.
However, we do not utilize ground truth and distorted image
pairs for training.
3.2. Implementation Details
Similar to [12], we utilize cross-connection architectures to
assimilate coarse-to-ﬁne generation in our ﬂow prediction
module U. Self-attention layers [18] are also used to incor-
porate global image information, since ﬁsheye distortion is
viewed as an image-level characteristic. The warping module
W is based on the Spatial Transformer Networks (STN) [19],
performing a fully-differentiable re-sampling operation.
Our discriminator D = (Dadv, Dcls) simply consists
of multiple down-sampling and strided-convolution layers.
Sharing initial layers, Dadv outputs real or fake labels while
Dcls outputs the class labels, i.e. distorted or distortion-free.
3.3. Quantitative Results
We evaluate the effectiveness of our model by comparing
with two state-of-the-art image translation methods, Cycle-
GAN [20] and UNIT [21]. As listed in Table 1, we compute
PSNR, SSIM, and RMSE between the recovered outputs
and the ground truth distortion-free images. From this table,
we see that our method performed favorably state-of-the-art
unpaired image translation methods across all datasets.

4. CONCLUSION
We presented a unique and novel self-supervised learning
model based on GAN, Fisheye GAN (FE-GAN), for rectify-
ing ﬁsh-eye distortion from a single image. The novelty of
our method lies in the unique design of geometric constraints
observed from the input distorted images and the predicted
distortion ﬂow (with the resulting output distortion-free im-
ages). Using only unpaired images without any ground truth
camera parameter supervision or requiring image pairs during
training, our experiments conﬁrmed that our model quantita-
tively outperformed state-of-the-arts image-translation based
deep models. More importantly, our FE-GAN was able to
produce satisfactory rectiﬁed distortion-free images than the
aforementioned methods did.
Therefore, the effectiveness
and robustness of our model can be successfully veriﬁed.
