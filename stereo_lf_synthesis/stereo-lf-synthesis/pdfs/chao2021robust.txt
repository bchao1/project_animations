ROBUST LIGHT FIELD SYNTHESIS FROM STEREO IMAGES WITH LEFT-RIGHT 
GEOMETRIC CONSISTENCY 
 
Chun-Hao Chao, Chang-Le Liu, and Homer H. Chen 
 
National Taiwan University 
 
ABSTRACT 
 
We propose a lightweight yet effective deep learning pipeline 
for light field synthesis from a single stereo image pair. Our 
pipeline consists of a convolutional network (CNN) that 
enforces a left-right consistency constraint on the light fields 
synthesized from left and right stereo views, a stage that 
merges light fields synthesized from left and right stereo 
views with a novel alpha blending technique, and a final 
refinement network using a unique 3D convolution operation. 
Our experiments quantitatively and qualitatively confirm the 
effectiveness and robustness of the proposed model, which 
performs favorably against state-of-the-art algorithms for 
light field synthesis from extremely sparse (only one, two, or 
four) views while using much fewer parameters.  
Index Terms— light field synthesis, light field 
reconstruction, stereo vision, CNN, deep learning 
 
1. INTRODUCTION 
 
Light field imaging allows users to perform digital refocusing 
or change the view angle of a scene, and are often acquired 
through plenoptic cameras [1] or camera arrays [2]. However, 
capturing high spatial and angular resolution light fields is a 
great challenge, since plenoptic cameras have inherent 
spatial-angular resolution tradeoff [1] and since dense camera 
arrays are impractical for end-users due to bulkiness and cost.  
Many techniques have been developed to address the 
problem of light field reconstruction from sparsely sampled 
views, or so-called “angular super-resolution” [3]–[5]. These 
models aim to increase the angular resolution of the sparsely 
sampled light fields, e.g. reconstruct a 7*7 light field by 
interpolating 3*3 sampled views. However, the underlying 
assumption that users are able to acquire an array of sampled 
views is not always true since such light field capturing 
devices are not easily accessible. 
At the other end of the spectrum, Srinivasan et al [6] 
investigated the problem of light field synthesis from a single 
RGB image. Although plausible visual results were obtained, 
reconstructing the absolute depth of a scene from a single 
image is an ill-posed problem [7]–[9]. Therefore, it would be 
hard to integrate the synthesis pipeline into applications (e.g. 
AR) that require absolute depth information. This limits the 
generalizability of the approach.  
In this work, we consider the problem of light field 
synthesis from stereo image pairs. This is analogous to 
synthesizing a high-resolution light field from only two 
horizontal light field views, which is much harder than light 
field reconstruction from a grid of sampled views. The 
solution to this problem will be valuable in the real world 
because, unlike the grid of sampled views, it is relatively easy 
to capture stereo image pairs either through stereo cameras or 
by translating a single-lens camera slightly in the horizontal 
direction. Furthermore, it is convenient to obtain absolute 
depth from stereo images [10]–[12] for various devices of 3D 
visual applications, such as AR headsets. 
To our knowledge, few works have addressed this 
problem. Zhang et al [13] proposed to use a phased-based 
synthesis technique to iteratively refine disparity maps 
between two input stereo views. Then the densely-spaced 
light field light field views are reconstructed by warping 
stereo images using the disparity map. However, this iterative 
approach is inherently slow. Also, it only uses the left view 
to generate light fields and does not exploit left-right 
information when synthesizing light fields. Zhou et al [14] 
developed the multiplane-images representation (MPI) 
technique and synthesized new views by performing 
homography and alpha composition on the multiplane images. 
However, this approach requires camera poses of the given 
stereo images and the views to be synthesized during both 
training and inference, which may not be available for light 
fields and stereo images in the wild.  
We propose an end-to-end deep learning framework to 
synthesize light field from stereo images. By exploiting 
geometric properties of left-right stereo images, our model 
learns to extrapolate and interpolate stereo images into coarse 
light fields.  It further handles occlusion boundaries and non-
Lambertian effects with a novel 3DCNN architecture to 
refine the results.  
The contributions of our work are highlighted as follows: 
• 
Our pipeline synthesizes high quality light field, which 
is made possible by exploiting left-right consistency of 
stereo images and distance-weighted alpha blending. 
• 
Our pipeline incorporates a novel patch-wise spatial 
angular-shuffling 3DCNN network to refine light field 
using much fewer parameters than prior methods. 
• 
Our pipeline generalizes well for real-world stereo 
images, and the synthesized light fields lead to promising 
refocused images.

3. EXPERIEMENTS AND RESULTS 
 
3.1. Datasets 
We evaluate our method on three publicly available datasets: 
INRIA Lytro 1G Dataset [17], HCI 4D Light Field Dataset 
[18], and the Stanford Light Field Archive [19]. We split 
INRIA, HCI and Stanford datasets into training/testing data 
and obtain 46/17, 20/4, 9/2 light fields, respectively. The 
above datasets have varying maximum disparity between 
adjacent light field views (INRIA dataset has small disparity 
while Stanford dataset has large disparity) and can thus help 
verify if our pipeline is robust to different disparity levels 
between adjacent light field views. 
 
3.2. Implementation Details 
For the disparity estimation network, we employ an 
architecture similar to the design by Srinivasan et al [6]. The 
final layer of all networks is a 𝑡𝑎𝑛ℎ function that scales 
output values to (-1, 1) to stabilize the training process. We 
use ELU [20] as our hidden layer activation function. All 
networks are trained end-to-end from scratch using the Adam 
optimization [21] algorithm with default parameters 𝛽, = 0.9, 
 𝛽( = 0.999, and 𝜖= 106. All models use the same loss 
function weights 𝑤0 = 1, 𝑤- = 0.001, 𝑤" = 1. All networks 
are trained for 2000 epochs with a learning rate of 0.001. We 
randomly extract 135×135 patches from light field views as 
training data.  
 
3.3. Quantitative Results 
Synthesized Light Field Quality 
We evaluate the effectiveness of our model by comparing it 
with three state-of-the-art light field synthesis methods that 
work for extremely sparse input views: Zhang et al [13] (from 
two views, non-learning based), Srinivasan et al [6] (from 
center view), and Kalantari et al [22] (from four corner 
views). Table 1 shows the PSNR and SSIM of the synthesized 
light fields. We can see that the proposed method outperforms 
the three state-of-the-art light field synthesis methods for all 
three datasets. 
Model Size and Inference Time 
We also compare the model size and the inference time to 
generate a 7 × 7 angular resolution light field of models 
trained on the INRIA dataset. As shown in Table 2, our model 
has 44% fewer parameters compared to Srinivasan et al, and 
is on par with Kalantari et al. Since our model synthesizes a 
whole light field in one pass, its inference time is much 
shorter than Kalantari et al, which synthesizes one view at a 
time. However, our model has longer inference time than 
Srinivasan et al, since we first synthesize light fields 𝐹! and 
𝐹" from left and right light field views, respectively, and then 
merge them.

Generalizability to Real-world Stereo Images 
Our model is trained on the INRIA dataset. To verify its 
generalizability to real-world images, we capture new stereo 
images by translating a smartphone camera slightly in the 
horizontal direction. Results in Figure 4 show that the 
resulting refocused images  are visually appealing. 
3.5. Ablation Studies 
Finally, we conduct ablation studies on models trained on 
INRIA [17] and HCI [18] datasets to verify the effectiveness  
of each component in our light field synthesis pipeline. We 
consider our pipeline excluding left-right consistency and 
disparity smoothness constraint. We also consider our 
pipeline using different light field blending methods and 
refinement network architectures. For the ablation study on 
network architecture, we compare our refinement network 
model with the 3DCNN architecture of Srinivasan et al. From 
the results in Table 3, we can see that each component in our 
light field synthesis pipeline is essential, which means our 
design is effective. 
4. CONCLUSION 
We present a lightweight yet effective pipeline to synthesize 
a light field from a single stereo image pair. The novelty of 
our method lies in the unique design that fully leverages 
information from left and right stereo images and a special 
3D convolution operation. From only two horizontal views, 
our pipeline is able to synthesize a 7 × 7 or 9 × 9 angular 
resolution light field. Our method qualitatively and 
quantitatively 
outperforms 
state-of-the-art 
light 
field 
synthesis methods that work for extremely sparse views (one, 
two or four views). Furthermore, experiments conducted on 
three light field datasets verify that our pipeline is robust to 
disparity levels between adjacent light field views. Finally, 
our pipeline generalizes well to real-world stereo images and 
can synthesize visually appealing light fields from stereo 
images in the wild.
