Abstract—Light field displays are widely regarded as the ultimate 3-D display as they provide a fully natural visual experience by 
reproducing light rays in space. However, many 3-D contents come in the form of stereo images instead of light fields. In this paper, 
we propose a system solution that generates light field data from stereo contents and furthermore compensates for the optical 
distortion of the light field display. It consists of a novel light field synthesis network that synthesizes a light field from a pair of stereo 
images and a compensation technique that rectifies the synthesized light field data to compensate for the optical distortion of the 
display. Our experiments quantitatively and qualitatively verify the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed light field synthesis 
method, which performs favorably against previous algorithms for light field synthesis from stereo images. Our experiments further 
show that the proposed method is generalizable to real-world image data captured by a smartphone camera. Finally, we verify through 
optics software simulation and real shots to show that our light field synthesis method is suitable for real-world light field displays and 
that our optical distortion compensation technique significantly improves the displayed light field quality. 
Index Terms—Light field display, augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), light field synthesis, CNN, deep learning
 
INTRODUCTION 
In recent years, light field display technology has drawn wide attention 
in the 3-D display and AR/VR communities, because it can resolve 
the notorious vergence-accommodation conflict that causes nausea 
and dizziness to users of most existing stereo displays. Unlike 
conventional 3-D displays that provide 3-D sensation by presenting an 
image to each eye, a light field display presents a light field to each 
eye. More specifically, it takes light field data as input, reproduces the 
light rays corresponding to the input, and projects them into the eyes. 
Light field display is often referred to as the ultimate display since it 
provides users with a natural and comfortable visual experience [1].  
In this paper, we focus on the conversion of stereo data to light 
field data for near-eye light field AR displays (that is, AR glasses). 
This work is motivated by two practical issues of light field display. 
The first one is related to the scarcity of light field contents. Given that 
stereo data are readily available or easy to obtain for content creators 
and developers, it makes sense to produce the light field contents from 
stereo data. The second issue, which is related to the first one, has to 
do with the deployment of light field technology. As the industry 
migrates from conventional AR display to light field AR display, it 
has to utilize the abundant existing contents that are in the form of

We approach the conversion of stereo data in a divide-and-conquer 
manner. Specifically, we divide it into two sub-tasks: light field 
synthesis and optical distortion compensation. This approach allows 
the flexibility needed to generate suitable contents for a light field 
display with its own optical characteristics. For light field synthesis, 
we propose an end-to-end deep learning framework that exploits the 
geometric property of left-right stereo images to extrapolate and 
interpolate stereo images into coarse light fields. Then it handles 
occlusion boundaries and non-Lambertian effects with a novel 3-D 
CNN architecture for refinement. We also develop a technique to 
speed up the light field synthesis, useful for time-critical applications. 
For optical distortion compensation, we remedy the effect of non-ideal 
optics of the display on light field quality. Specifically, we perform a 
ray transfer matrix analysis on the optical paths of our light field 
display and design a method to compensate for lens distortion and 
optical misalignment of the display. This allows a virtual object at any 
depth to appear sharp and clear to the user when focused on. 
The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. 
• 
We propose a solution that enables conventional 3-D stereo 
contents to be converted to a format suitable for a near-eye light 
field display. 
• 
We propose a novel synthesis network that synthesizes a light field 
from a pair of stereo images and develop a speedup version of the

light field quality or synthesis speed. 
•

1 RELATED WORK 
Our work is closely related to light field synthesis, 3-D displays, 
and optical distortion compensation for light field displays. This 
section reviews related work. 
1.1 
Light Field Synthesis 
Light fields are often acquired through plenoptic cameras [2] or 
camera arrays [3]. However, capturing high spatial and angular 
resolution light fields with such devices is a great challenge, since 
plenoptic cameras have inherent spatial-angular resolution tradeoff [2] 
and dense camera arrays are bulk and costly.  
Many techniques have been developed for light field 
reconstruction from sparsely sampled light field subviews, 
collectively called “angular super-resolution” [4]–[6]. These 
techniques aim to increase the angular resolution of the sparsely 
sampled light fields from, for example, 3×3 to 7×7 by view 
interpolation. Srinivasan et al [7] investigated light field synthesis 
from a single RGB image. Although plausible visual results were 
obtained, reconstructing the absolute depth of a scene from a single 
image is an ill-posed problem [8]–[10]. Thus, the generalizability of 
the approach is limited.  
Light field synthesis from a stereo image pair is harder than that 
from a grid of sampled subviews. But the solution to this problem 
would find more applications because, unlike the grid of sampled 
subviews, stereo image pairs are relatively easy to capture through 
stereo cameras or by translating a single-lens camera slightly in the 
horizontal direction [11]–[13]. 
Zhang et al [14] proposed a phased-based synthesis technique to 
iteratively refine disparity maps between two input stereo images. 
Then an array of densely spaced subviews is reconstructed by warping 
the stereo images using the resulting disparity map. However, this 
iterative approach is inherently slow. Zhou et al [15] developed a 
layered representation called multiplane images to synthesize new 
subviews by homography and alpha composition. This approach 
involves estimating the camera poses of the given stereo images and 
the subviews to be synthesized and hence requires additional overhead. 
The two methods synthesize one novel subview at a time and require 
multiple passes to synthesize a dense light field. This significantly 
limits their real-time synthesis capability. 
In contrast, our light field synthesis method does not require 
camera pose information for training and inference. In addition, it 
directly synthesizes a full dense light field in one forward pass. Also, 
our method fully leverages the geometry information of left-right 
stereo images through a left-right consistency loss and a novel alpha-
blending technique.

to create 3-D sensation through eye convergence. However, rendering 
the virtual images on a fixed focal plane may cause user fatigue due 
to the vergence-accommodation conflict. To address the problem, 
various approaches such as varifocal display [16]–[18], multifocal 
display [19]–[21], holographic display [22]–[24], volumetric display 
[25], and light field display (LFD) [26]–[28] have been proposed. It is 
worth noting that light field displays provide 3-D sensation by 
projecting light rays of the virtual object into eyes without any 
switchable element or eye tracker required by varifocal and multifocal

1.3 
Compensation for Optical Distortion 
The engineering design of an optical system is often an art of tradeoffs, 
and the manufacturing of optical devices is a complex and error-prone 
process. Unwanted lens aberration and optical misalignment are 
unavoidable. Lens aberration introduces distortion to the subview 
signal, and misalignment of optical components causes the subviews 
to deviate from the standard position and orientation. Such optical 
imperfection and misalignment lead to blur and distorted virtual 
objects. Many methods that involve pre-warping the input image have 
been developed to compensate for the lens distortion of stereoscopic 
3D displays [29], [30] and can be applied to light field displays. Unlike 
the lens distortion, which is intrinsic in nature, the subview 
misalignment extrinsically varies with the depth of the virtual object. 
Marco et al. [31] and Marton et al. [32] addressed the optical 
misalignment by adjustment of the projector lenses via global 
optimization. In our case, however, the projector lenses are not 
adjustable once the display is built. Under this circumstance, one has 
to resort to computational techniques to resolve the misalignment.

3 SPEEDUP OF LIGHT FIELD SYNTHESIS 
In this section, we discuss the improvement of the computational 
efficiency of the model described in Section 2. Our goal is to make the 
inference speed close to real-time. It is achieved by modifying each 
component of the method, including replacing the disparity estimation 
network, merging the light fields more efficiently, and removing the 
refinement network. The modified synthesis model serves as an 
alternative solution when synthesis speed is preferred over image 
quality. 
3.1 
Speeding up Disparity Estimation 
We replace the disparity estimation network defined in Section 2 with 
a modified 3-stage network based on the design by Wang et al. [35]. 
The spatial propagation network, which is implemented as the 4th stage 
of the original network defined in [35], is removed in the modified 
network to improve the efficiency.  
With this efficient network model, the disparity estimation 
component of the light field synthesis method can be performed in 
real-time. This comes at the cost of image quality, especially for the 
disparity map. However, since the depth of the image can still be 
identified clearly, this only causes a slight prediction error in the 
warped light field, as shown in Section 5.  
3.2 
Merging Light Fields by Half Blending 
In Section 2, distance-weighted alpha blending is used to merge the 
two separate coarse light fields 𝐹! and 𝐹". This process ensures that the 
prediction error of the merged light field is kept to a minimum. 
However, depending on the angular resolution of the light field, 
warping two light fields completely with full 𝑁× 𝑁sub-images may 
greatly increase the total operation time of the synthesis method. This 
can be improved with a modified merging method referred to as half 
blending. 
As its name suggests, half blending only warps one-half of sub-

4 COMPENSATION FOR OPTICAL DISTORTION 
The near-eye light field display considered in this work consists of an 
array of 𝑁× 𝑁 projectors, each projecting a subview 𝑉(𝑖, 𝑗) to the 
user’s eye through a light guide. To achieve optimal quality for the 
light field, we need to compensate for its optical distortion, which can 
be divided into lens distortion and optical misalignment. The lens 
distortion causes image deformation, while the optical misalignment 
causes inaccurate shift between light field subviews and results in poor 
refocusing.

5 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP AND RESULTS 
5.1 
Evaluation of Light Field Synthesis 
5.1.1 
Datasets and Evaluation Metrics 
We use three publicly available datasets, INRIA Lytro 1G Dataset [37], 
INRIA Synthetic Dense Light Field Dataset [38], and the HCI 4D 
Light Field Dataset [39], and split each of them into training/testing 
data, resulting in 46/17, 20/4, 32/7 light fields, respectively, for these 
three datasets. The angular resolutions of the three light field datasets 
are 7×7, 9×9, and 9×9, respectively, and the spatial resolutions are 
371×371, 512×512, and 512×512, respectively. 
We use two quality assessment metrics, peak signal-to-noise-ratio 
(PSNR) and structural similarity index measure (SSIM), to evaluate 
the quality of the light fields synthesized by our method.

5.2 
Evaluation of Speedup 
Our proposed speedup model is compared with the original light field 
synthesis model trained on the HCI Dataset. Tables 8 and 9 show the 
techniques described in Section 3 improve the inference time and 
parameter count. We can see that, at the cost of a slight quality drop, 
these techniques collectively cut down more than one-half of the 
running time, achieving a frame rate close to 15 fps. 
In addition, due to the lightweight property of the disparity 
estimation network, the modified method has a far smaller parameter 
count than the typical 3-D CNN. This means our light field synthesis 
method can run on low-power devices, like mobile phones or AR/VR 
glasses. Overall, the tradeoff provided by these modifications is well 
justified for live or online applications, for which speed matters.

6 CONCLUSION 
In this paper, we propose a system solution that enables conventional 
stereo contents to be converted to light fields and presented on a near-
eye light field display. Our method consists of two components: a 
novel light field synthesis network and a compensation technique that 
rectifies the light field data to compensate for the optical distortion of 
the display.  
The novelty of our light field synthesis method lies in the unique 
design that fully leverages information from left and right stereo 
images and a special 3-D convolution operation. From only two 
horizontal views, our method can synthesize 7×7 or 9×9 angular 
resolution light fields. Our method qualitatively and quantitatively 
outperforms previous light field synthesis methods that work for 
stereo images. Furthermore, experiments using different input 
configurations verify that our method is robust to disparity levels of 
the input stereo images. Finally, our method can also synthesize a light 
field from images captured using a mobile phone. 
We also provide a speedup version of the light field synthesis 
network that allows users to prioritize speed over synthesis quality. 
Finally, by pre-warping and pre-shifting the synthesized light fields to 
compensate for the optical distortion induced by the imperfection of a 
light field display, the synthesized light fields can be correctly 
displayed on a light field display prototype.
